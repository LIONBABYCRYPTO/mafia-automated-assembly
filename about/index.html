About Werewolf

An open-source social deduction platform designed to bring people together through the timeless art of bluffing, reasoning, and reading the room.

Our Mission

Werewolf has been played around campfires, in classrooms, and at parties for decades. Our mission is simple: make it accessible to everyone, anywhere, without friction.

No app downloads. No account creation. No paywalls. Just scan a QR code and play. We believe the best technology is the kind you don't notice — the kind that gets out of the way and lets people connect.

The Story Behind the Game

The original Werewolf (also known as Mafia) was created in 1986 by Dmitry Davidoff, a psychology student at Moscow State University. The game was inspired by the paranoia and distrust of Cold War-era politics, where ordinary citizens lived alongside hidden threats.

What started as a student experiment in social dynamics spread across university campuses worldwide, evolving through countless variations and house rules. Today, it's played in over 50 countries and has inspired video games, TV shows, and corporate training programs.

This digital version was built to capture the magic of in-person play while removing the administrative burden. No more shuffling cards, counting votes by hand, or managing complex role assignments. The technology handles the mechanics so the host can focus on what matters: creating a great experience for their players.

Why We Built This

Running Werewolf for large groups is logistics-heavy. The host needs to:

  • Print and shuffle role cards
  • Secretly distribute roles without anyone seeing
  • Manage night actions in the right order
  • Count votes accurately
  • Track eliminations across multiple rounds

One mistake and the game is compromised. We built this platform to eliminate those friction points. The host's only job is to manage the room and read the atmosphere — the system handles everything else.

The project is open source (MIT license), meaning anyone can inspect the code, suggest improvements, or self-host their own instance. We believe transparency is essential for trust, especially in a game about deception.

Technical Overview

Built for reliability and scale, this platform handles games of any size without breaking a sweat.

  • Frontend: Pure HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript — no framework dependencies, instant loading.
  • Backend: Cloudflare Workers for serverless API endpoints with global low-latency access.
  • Storage: Cloudflare KV with automatic TTL-based cleanup. Game data self-destructs after 24 hours.
  • Hosting: GitHub Pages for static assets, Cloudflare Workers for dynamic API logic.
  • Source Code: Fully open source under MIT license on GitHub. Contributions welcome.
